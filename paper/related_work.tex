\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related_work}
\subsection{Detection and Corpus Reliability}
Phishing-email detection evolved from engineered header, link, and text
features with supervised classifiers \cite{fette2007learning}
to diverse machine-learning and neural approaches reviewed by Kavya and
Sumathi \cite{kavya2025review}. However, corpus-specific formatting,
collection period, source overlap, and ambiguous class definitions
can inflate in-domain results. Spam is not necessarily phishing;
phishing-only collections cannot measure false positives on benign mail.
Pajola \emph{et al.} \cite{pajola2025ephishgen}
reassessed legacy evaluations and released the multilingual
E-PhishLLM benchmark of 16,616 synthetic messages through E-PhishGEN.
Bhuiyan and Bhuiyan \cite{bhuiyan2026fragility}
subsequently investigated leave-one-corpus-out transfer,
source artifacts, prevalence shift, and alert budgets. Thus,
source shift and shortcut diagnosis are critical methodological
controls rather than stand-alone novelty.

\subsection{LLM-Written and Rephrased Email}
Heiding \emph{et al.} \cite{heiding2024devising} analyzed both the design and detection of LLM-generated phishing with human participants, establishing the dual-use context for email security. Weinz \emph{et al.} \cite{weinz2025impact} studied LLM-assisted
phishing in organizational simulations. Opara \emph{et al.}
\cite{opara2025stylometry} tested commercial mail filters
and introduced stylometric detection using 47 features. Importantly,
authorship inference (AI versus human) is not identical to maliciousness
inference (phishing versus legitimate). The open-domain MAGE study
\cite{li2024mage} reports cross-generator weaknesses in
machine-text detection, but does not establish phishing
classification performance.

Ferrell \emph{et al.} \cite{ferrell2025rephrasing}
tested LLM rephrasings of both phishing and safe emails, finding
performance reductions and improvement after retraining.
Nance \emph{et al.} \cite{nance2025augmentation} independently investigated LLM-based data augmentation for phishing email detection. Kulal \emph{et al.} \cite{kulal2025robust} assessed preprocessing
against LLM-generated and adversarial email. These studies rule
out claims that ordinary rephrasing experiments or augmented
training are new.

\subsection{Generator Transfer, Calibration, and Provenance}
Gutierrez \emph{et al.} \cite{gutierrez2026crossmodel}
used phishing-only human and LLM examples from GPT-4.1,
DeepSeek 3.2, and Llama 3.3 70B. Their stylometric classifier
exhibited a pronounced cross-generator F1 gap despite strong
ranking ability; threshold adaptation using some target labels
narrowed that gap. Their experimental labels indicate
AI-versus-human origin \emph{among phishing messages}, not
phishing-versus-legitimate intent. Therefore, their results
cannot quantify false alarms on legitimate AI-written mail,
and target-label calibration is not a zero-shot result.
Predictive confidence itself requires explicit calibration
analysis \cite{guo2017calibration}. We consider generator
identity a stratification variable and prohibit test-target
threshold selection.

\subsection{Pairwise Robustness and Operational Risk}
Toth \emph{et al.} \cite{toth2026phishfuzzer} released PhishFuzzer
with 3,300 seed emails and 19,800 linked variants spanning
phishing, spam, and valid mail, including sender, URL, and
attachment metadata. Parent linking supports paired robustness
analysis, but editing these artifacts can alter security
semantics: a shared parent does not prove a same-label pair.
Independent review is essential before declaring an edit
style-preserving or label-changing.

Arharbi and El Qadi \cite{arharbi2026shifts} already explored
generative shift, prediction stability, DistilBERT,
and SHAP/entropy-based mitigation. Risk--coverage trade-offs
are also established in selective classification
\cite{geifman2019selectivenet}. Therefore, neither
shift-robust DistilBERT nor abstention alone constitutes
novelty. Realized test false-positive rates, review workload,
and phishing recall at preregistered operating thresholds
must be reported alongside PR-AUC
\cite{saito2015precision}.

\subsection{Positioning of the Present Research}
Table~\ref{tab:related_comparison} contrasts existing
studies by \emph{task} and evaluation, avoiding comparisons
of accuracy across incompatible benchmarks. The proposed
research investigates two separate desiderata for
phishing-versus-legitimate classification:
stability under \emph{human-verified security-equivalent}
rewrites, and sensitivity to \emph{adjudicated
security-changing} counterfactuals. Proposed learning
objectives must be compared with classification-only,
ordinary augmentation, same-label consistency, and
matched-budget strong controls. Joint generator and
corpus holdouts will be attempted only if provenance
permits them. No performance advantage or novel
algorithm is asserted without such evidence.
